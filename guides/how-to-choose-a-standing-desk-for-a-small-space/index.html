How to Choose a Standing Desk for a Small Space

By Lucas, founder·Updated October 2026

A standing desk can make a small home office feel more flexible, but the wrong one will eat your room and wobble every time you type. Here is what actually matters when space is tight.

Measure before you shop

Measure the wall or corner where the desk will go, and leave about 3 feet (90 cm) behind it for your chair. In small rooms, desktops around 40–48 inches wide by 24 inches deep usually fit best. Anything shallower starts to push your monitor too close to your eyes.

Check the height range for your body

When standing, your elbows should sit at roughly 90 degrees with the desktop at elbow height. Taller people need a higher maximum; shorter people need a lower minimum for sitting. Compare your own elbow height to the desk's listed range instead of trusting "fits most people."

Single vs. dual motor

Single-motor desks are cheaper and fine for a laptop and one monitor. Dual-motor desks lift more weight, move more smoothly and tend to last longer. If you use two monitors or a heavy monitor arm, dual motor is worth it.

Stability is the hidden deal-breaker

Small desks wobble more at standing height. Look for a crossbar or a thick frame, and search owner reviews for words like "wobble" or "shake" at full height.

Tip: wobble gets worse the higher the desk goes, so taller users should weigh stability above almost everything else.

Consider an L-shape or a converter

For corners, a compact L-shaped standing desk uses space that would otherwise be wasted. If you can't replace your current desk, a desktop converter that sits on top is the cheapest and smallest option.

Quick checklist

  • Fits your measured space with room for the chair
  • Height range matches your elbow height
  • Dual motor if you have heavy equipment
  • Owner reviews mention good stability
  • Memory presets, cable tray and anti-collision
