What tall people should check before buying

  • Seat height. Sitting with feet flat and knees at about 90°, measure from the floor to the back of your knee. Your chair's top seat height should be at or above that number. Over about 6'3", look for 21 in or more, or a tall-cylinder option.
  • Seat depth. You want about two to three finger-widths between the seat edge and the back of your knee. Short seats leave long thighs unsupported; a seat slider fixes that.
  • Backrest and headrest. On a long torso, fixed lumbar ends up too low. A height-adjustable back or lumbar, plus a headrest if you recline, makes a bigger difference than extra padding.
  • Desk height matters too. Raising the chair only works if your desk goes up with it. Our standing desks for small spaces guide lists height ranges, and a monitor arm gets the screen up to eye level.
  • Check capacity and warranty. Tall people are often heavier too. A higher weight rating and a long warranty usually point to a sturdier base and gas lift.
