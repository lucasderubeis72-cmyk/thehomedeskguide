What to look for in a budget ergonomic chair

  • Lumbar you can position. A pad that sits at the wrong height is worse than none. Look for lumbar that adjusts up and down, or a backrest that moves with you, like the Doro C300 or FlexiSpot OC5.
  • Armrests that reach your desk. If the armrests can't drop low enough or slide close enough, you'll end up perched forward. 3D armrests (up/down, in/out, rotate) make the biggest difference for typing.
  • Seat height vs. desk height. Your elbows should sit roughly level with the desk. If you use a standing desk, set the desk to your chair, not the other way around. See our standing desks for small spaces guide.
  • Mesh vs. foam seat. Mesh stays cooler; foam feels softer at first. If your room runs warm, mesh is the safer choice.
  • Weight rating and returns. Choose a chair rated comfortably above your weight, and buy where returns are easy. Comfort is personal, and a week of real use tells you more than any spec sheet.
