Before you buy a monitor arm

  • Check VESA holes. Look at the back of your monitor for a 75×75 or 100×100 mm screw pattern. No holes means you need an adapter, and some curved screens need a special bracket.
  • Match the weight range, both ends. Weigh the monitor without its stand. Gas-spring arms need a minimum load too; a screen that's too light makes the arm drift up.
  • Measure your desk edge. Clamps fit a limited desktop thickness, and thin particleboard or glass tops can crack. A grommet mount through a cable hole spreads the load better.
  • Standing desks need lighter loads. An arm moves its weight to the back edge, so stay within your desk's rating and avoid the wobble that comes from a heavy screen on a long reach. Our standing desks for small spaces guide lists desk sizes and motors.
  • Plan for cables. Leave a little slack at each joint so the arm can move without pulling cables loose.
