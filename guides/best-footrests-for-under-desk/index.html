How to choose an under-desk footrest

  • Figure out the height you need first. Sit with your chair set so your elbows line up with the desk. If your feet don't rest flat with your thighs roughly level, measure the gap between your heels and the floor. That number is the footrest height you need.
  • Adjustable beats guessing. If you're not sure of that gap, or more than one person uses the desk, a model with several heights (Fellowes, Mount-It, ErgoFoam) is the safer buy.
  • Rigid or foam? Plastic and wood platforms hold their height for years and tilt to change ankle angle. Foam is softer for bare feet but slowly compresses and has little or no adjustment.
  • A footrest can't fix a chair that's too tall. If you need more than about 6–7 in of lift, look at your chair and desk height too. Our ergonomic chairs under $300 guide lists seat height ranges for each chair.
  • Check the floor. On hardwood or a chair mat, look for rubber feet or a grippy base so the footrest doesn't slide away when you push against it.
