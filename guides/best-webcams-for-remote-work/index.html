How to choose a webcam for remote work

  • Lighting matters more than resolution. A 1080p camera in a well-lit room looks better than a 4K camera in a dark one. Face a window or put a lamp behind your screen before spending more; our desk lamps for home office guide covers soft, adjustable options.
  • 4K is mostly about headroom. Zoom and Teams usually send 1080p or less, so 4K mainly helps with sharper cropping, digital zoom and recordings. If you only take calls, a good 1080p or 2K camera is enough.
  • Check the field of view. Around 65–78° frames one person at a desk. Wider settings (90°+) show more of the room, which is useful for two people or a whiteboard but also shows the clutter behind you.
  • Look for a built-in privacy cover. A sliding or twisting shutter is easier to use every day than a clip you have to keep track of.
  • Mount it at eye level. A webcam clipped to a laptop lid looks up your nose. Put the laptop on a stand or the camera on a monitor at eye height; a monitor arm makes it easy to get the screen and camera to the right height.
